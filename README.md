# Catholic Cross References

A cross-reference system for the Catholic Bible, built in the open and given to the Church as public domain — anyone may use it freely, for anything, forever.

Modern Catholic Bibles are nearly bare of cross references in the seven books the whole Church read as Scripture for fifteen centuries — Tobit, Judith, Wisdom, Sirach (Ecclesiasticus), Baruch, and 1–2 Maccabees — and in the added chapters of Esther and Daniel. The big reference collections used by most Bible apps cover only the 66 books of the Protestant canon. This project exists to close that gap: first by recovering the references the Catholic Bible's own oldest English translation printed in its margins, then by building a new, carefully checked apparatus of our own on top of them, book by book.

It already feeds the free [Catholic Bible app](https://jesuslovestyler.github.io/Catholic-Bible/) (Douay-Rheims, Challoner revision), where the seed below now serves the deuterocanon books and added chapters beside the Treasury of Scripture Knowledge.

## Version 0.15.0 — Isaias, Jeremias, Lamentations, Ezechiel, and Daniel chapters 1-12 only

**Isaias, Jeremias, Lamentations, Ezechiel, and Daniel chapters 1-12 only — 143 new references** (16 parallel, 96 allusion, 27 quotation, 4 theme). `our-references.json` now holds **1277 references** (348 parallel, 751 allusion, 120 quotation, 58 theme).

The great citations are completed verse by verse where the seed carried only their opening: Matthew's long Servant citation at Isaias 42:2–4, Luke's Nazareth reading at 61:2 and 58:6, the Ethiopian's second verse at 53:8, and Hebrews 8's new-covenant citation at Jeremias 31:32, 31:33 and 31:34; Daniel's Son of man is likewise gathered at every uncarried coming — Matthew 24:30, Luke 21:27, Acts 7:55, and the Apocalypse's 1:7, 1:13 and 14:14 — while John's closing visions take up Ezechiel's river and tree of life (47:1, 47:12) and Daniel's opened books and judgement given to the saints. The gaps are stated plainly: Lamentations yields a single new entry (3:30 → Isaias 50:6), its chapters 2 and 5 nothing that survives checking, and whole runs of Isaias, Jeremias, Ezechiel and Daniel — Daniel 13–14 and the additions not redone, having been completed at stage 2 — are left empty rather than padded; Matthew 2:23's Nazarene saying and John 7:38's unnamed citation were refused, because no verse in this corpus prints the words they claim. No draft entry was removed at merge: every one of the 143 verified against the text and none collided with a pair an earlier group of this run had already carried.

**Job, Proverbs, Ecclesiastes, Canticle of Canticles — 80 new references** (6 parallel, 64 allusion, 9 quotation, 1 theme). `our-references.json` now holds **1134 references** (332 parallel, 655 allusion, 93 quotation, 54 theme).

Job's words prove to have a long afterlife: the Preacher restates his naked confession entire (Ecclesiastes 5:14 → Job 1:21), Paul closes his doxology with the whirlwind's unanswerable question (Romans 11:35 → Job 41:2), and his resurrection confession is taken up in Isaias 26:19 (→ Job 19:25), while what Job confesses of God alone — going upon the waves of the sea — the Lord does on the lake (Matthew 14:25 → Job 9:8). Proverbs is completed where the seed carried only a first half or a chapter pointer: Hebrews 12:6 supplies the unkeyed half of the discipline proverb (→ Proverbs 3:12), Romans 12:20 the coals half of the enemy-feeding proverb (→ 25:22), and 1 Peter 4:8 the proverb's clause entire (→ 10:12); the Canticle, long bare, is keyed where later texts actually take up its words — the knocking beloved of 5:2 behind Apocalypse 3:20, the woman fair as the moon and elect as the sun (6:9) behind Apocalypse 12:1, and the bride without spot (4:7) behind Ephesians 5:27. The gaps are stated plainly: whole runs of Job's speeches, several Proverbs chapters, Ecclesiastes 6, and the Canticle's middle chapters yield nothing a later text anchors in words and are left empty rather than padded; Proverbs 8's Wisdom at creation is not keyed to John 1 or Colossians 1, an identification this corpus's wording does not print, and James 4:14's vapour is not keyed to the Preacher's vanity, because Challoner prints two different words. One further draft entry was removed at merge because an earlier group of this same run had already carried it into our layer.

## Version 0.12.0 — 3 Kings, 4 Kings, 1 Paralipomenon, 2 Paralipomenon, Esdras, Nehemias, and the Hebrew chapters of Esther

**3 Kings, 4 Kings, 1 Paralipomenon, 2 Paralipomenon, Esdras, Nehemias, and the Hebrew chapters of Esther — 100 new references** (48 parallel, 43 allusion, 8 quotation, 1 theme). `our-references.json` now holds **962 references** (303 parallel, 560 allusion, 46 quotation, 53 theme).

The Elias and Eliseus thread is gathered whole: Jesus and James on the shut heaven are anchored at the drought oracle itself (3 Kings 17:1) and at the rain's falling (18:45), Paul's quotation is keyed to the divine answer on Horeb (Romans 11:4 → 3 Kings 19:18), and the one clause of Hebrews 11:35 — "women received of resurrection their dead" — is keyed separately to each raising behind it, the widow of Sarepta's son and the Sunamite's. Chronicles is mapped to Kings and Samuel verse by verse only where a verse carries distinct substance — the dedication prayer in both tellings, the found Book, Josias's Passover — with the discrepancies stated, not harmonised: Ochozias is forty-two at his accession in Chronicles and twenty-two in Kings, as printed, and Isaias's Babylonian envoy is Merodach where Kings prints Berodach. The return from exile closes its own circle, Esdras opening by quoting the Cyrus decree with which Chronicles ends (1 Esdras 1:2 → 2 Paralipomenon 36:23), and Esther's Hebrew chapters carry the Joseph pattern for Mardochai — Pharaoh's ring and procession become Assuerus's — while Aman's stock is traced to Agag, the Amalecite Saul spared. The gaps are stated plainly: Manasses' captivity and repentance stand only in Chronicles, with no Kings verse to pair and none invented; the famous "if my people shall humble themselves" (2 Paralipomenon 7:14) has no New Testament counterpart that can be quoted from this corpus and was refused; and Esther 4:14's providence — "who knoweth whether thou art not come to the kingdom for this time" — has no verbal anchor in any earlier text, so it is left unkeyed rather than forced. Two further draft entries were removed at merge because an earlier group of this same run had already carried them into our layer.

## Version 0.11.0 — Josue, Judges, Ruth, 1 Kings, 2 Kings

**Josue, Judges, Ruth, 1 Kings, 2 Kings — 45 new references** (35 allusion, 10 parallel). `our-references.json` now holds **862 references** (255 parallel, 517 allusion, 38 quotation, 52 theme).

The strongest thread is Anna's canticle behind Mary's: the Magnificat's deposing and exalting and its hungry filled and rich sent empty are keyed verse by verse to 1 Kings 2:7–8, the song of the barren woman who received Samuel. The gaps are stated plainly: Josue 24:15 ("choose this day") and Ruth 1:16–17 ("thy people my people") are much quoted in devotion but no later verse in this corpus takes up their wording with a verifiable anchor, so both are left unkeyed rather than forced, and Barac and Jephte receive no key in this stage — Hebrews 11:32 names them in a list with no verse-level deed attached.

## Version 0.10.0 — Deuteronomy: the Torah complete

**Deuteronomy — 68 new references** (49 allusion, 10 parallel, 9 quotation). `our-references.json` now holds **817 references** (245 parallel, 482 allusion, 38 quotation, 52 theme). **With this version, our own layer covers the whole Torah** — Genesis, Exodus, Leviticus, Numbers, Deuteronomy — alongside the complete deuterocanon.

Deuteronomy's famous web proved to be almost entirely the old margins' already — all three of the Lord's temptation answers, the great commandment, the prophet like Moses in Acts 3, "vengeance is mine." What was missing is surprising: Galatians 3:10 quotes the last of Ebal's curses — "Cursed be he that abideth not in the words of this law" (27:26) — the verse Paul builds the argument on, and it was in no margin we have; Romans 10:8 completes Paul's "the word is nigh thee" at 30:14, where the seed carried only the ascent half; Mark 12:29 keys Jesus' first word of the answer — the Shema itself, 6:4 — where the seed keyed only 6:5. Also new: "the poor you have always with you" traced to 15:11; the root of bitterness (Heb 12:15 → 29:18); God pleading with Israel out of His own divorce law (Jer 3:1 → 24:4); and the Song of Moses mapped verse by verse into the prophets and the Apocalypse. One famous candidate was refused on principle: Hebrews 1:6's "let all the angels of God adore him" stands in the Greek of 32:43 but **not in the Douay-Rheims text this dataset is built on** — and a reference cannot cite words its own Bible does not print.

## Version 0.9.0 — Numbers

**Numbers — 68 new references** (50 allusion, 14 parallel, 2 quotation, 2 theme). `our-references.json` now holds **749 references** (235 parallel, 433 allusion, 29 quotation, 52 theme).

The seed's Numbers margins looked generous — 58 pairs — until inspected: a large share are whole-chapter pointers with no verse at all, and the sharpest verse of all was missing. Hebrews 9:13, the red heifer's ashes, is keyed by the seed to three Leviticus verses but never to the heifer's own law; 19:9 now carries it. Also new: the great processional psalm opening with Moses' cry at the rising of the ark (Ps 67:2 → 10:35); the Magi's star traced to Balaam's star risen out of Jacob (Matt 2:2 → 24:17); "sheep without a shepherd" traced to Moses' prayer for a successor (Matt 9:36 → 27:17, with 3 Kings 22:17 and Ezechiel 34:5); Jeremias turning Sehon's fire back on Moab in the ancient Hesebon proverb (48:45 → 21:28–29); and Moses' wish over Eldad and Medad — "O that all the people might prophesy" — become Joel's promise of the Spirit on all flesh (Joel 2:28 → 11:29). Paul's reckoning at Phogor is entered with its tension stated, not smoothed: 1 Corinthians 10:8 counts 23,000 fallen in one day; Numbers 25:9 gives the plague's whole toll, 24,000. Thirteen chapters (the censuses, the jealousy ordeal, the feast tariffs) honestly yield nothing a later text anchors to a verse.

## Version 0.7.0 — Exodus

**Exodus — 104 new references** (59 allusion, 25 parallel, 12 quotation, 8 theme), all keyed at the later verses that quote, retell, or argue from Exodus, pointing back. `our-references.json` now holds **606 references** (205 parallel, 334 allusion, 23 quotation, 44 theme).

Exodus, unlike Genesis, earns true quotations: Stephen's speech in Acts 7 is a spine of ten entries, carrying the burning bush itself (7:32 — the Gospels' own citations of Exodus 3:6 were already in the seed, and are not duplicated); James cites the commandments; and Numbers 14:18 quotes the Sinai proclamation back to God, Moses pleading God's own words. ## Version 0.5.0 — the deuterocanon complete

The last four works are in, and `our-references.json` now holds **380 references** (137 parallel, 207 allusion, 11 quotation, 25 theme) covering the whole deuterocanon — the part of the Catholic Bible where cross references were scarcest:

- **Baruch — 44 references**, and not one of them a parallel: Baruch is prayer and poetry reworking earlier texts, so its links are verbal. ## Version 0.1.0 — the seed

The seed is the cross-reference apparatus printed in the margins of the **original Douay-Rheims Bible** — the New Testament at Rheims in 1582 and the Old Testament at Douay in 1609–1610 — parsed into structured data.

- **1,984** marginal entries read
- **2,813** references kept, at **1,889** verses in **65** books
- **85** entry parts set aside, every one recorded with its reason in `dropped-entries.json`

The seed data comes from the [Original Douay-Rheims project](https://thedouayrheims.com), whose transcription of the original Bible (text and marginal apparatus) is released **CC0 1.0 / public domain** — corpus: `janvier-s/original-douay-rheims`, commit `d82b285`, folder `bible/tagged/`. This repository's own work is under the **Unlicense** (see `LICENSE`): public domain as nearly as the law allows.

### Files

- `cross-references.json` — the seed dataset. References are keyed by book (Douay-Rheims names and numbering, Psalms in Vulgate numbering), then by `"chapter:verse"`. Each entry keeps the margin's printed string (`raw`) beside its parsed `targets`, so every line can be traced back to the page it came from. A target with no verse (e.g. `"Genesis 22"`) cites the whole chapter, as the margin printed it.
- `our-references.json` — this project's own references, built on top of the seed (see the 0.2.0 section above). Same keying; each reference is labeled by kind and carries the quoted evidence from both passages.
- `dropped-entries.json` — everything that was **not** carried into the dataset, with the reason in plain words. Nothing was guessed to make the numbers bigger.
- `tools/parse_seed.py` — the parser that produced both files from the CC0 corpus, so the work can be re-run and checked.

Targets in **3 Esdras, 4 Esdras, and the Prayer of Manasses** — books the Douay Bible printed in its appendix, not among the 73 of the Catholic canon — are kept and flagged with `"appendix_targets": true`, exactly as they stand in the original.

## How the margins were read

The old margins cite in a shorthand of their time — `Sap. 7, 26.` for Wisdom 7:26, `3. Reg. 8.` for 3 Kings 8, `Eccli. 44. 22.` for Ecclesiasticus 44:22. They were read by these stated conventions, drawn from the corpus's own usage and applied uniformly:

- A comma joins chapter and verse (`Sap. 7, 26`); `v.` introduces verse numbers (`Iere. 25. v. 8. 9.`).
- Exactly one further plain number after a chapter reads as that chapter's verse (`Eccli. 44. 22`); a run of three or more plain numbers reads as a list of chapters (`Exo. 7. 8. 9. 10. 11. 12, 37`), and numbers joined by `&` are chapters.
- After a verse, a further plain number that fits the same chapter's verses is another verse of that chapter (`1. Mach. 4, 56. 59.`); a comma opens a fresh chapter-and-verse group.
- `Eccl.` with a chapter of 13 or more is Ecclesiasticus (Ecclesiastes has only 12 chapters); with a chapter of 12 or under it is genuinely ambiguous between the two books, and was **dropped rather than guessed**.
- Every target was checked against the original text itself — the book, the chapter, and the verse must exist in the Douay-Rheims as printed. Targets that fail (a handful, including a few misprints in the transcription) are in `dropped-entries.json`.

Before this dataset was published, its deuterocanon counts were cross-checked against an independent parse made for the Catholic Bible app: both readings agree exactly (Ecclesiasticus 128 entries → 150 references; Wisdom 34 → 43; Tobias 22 entries → 1 reference with 21 annotation markers set aside; Judith 14; Baruch 10; 1 Machabees 19; 2 Machabees 9).

## Where this goes

The seed is a beginning, not the harvest — four-century-old margins were sparing. On top of it, this project is building **our own cross references**, book by book, starting with the deuterocanon and the added chapters where the need is greatest. The deuterocanon is complete (0.2.0–0.5.0, above) — the ground where the need was greatest is covered first, as planned. From there the same patient work has begun extending, book by book, across the rest of the canon, starting with Genesis (0.6.0, above); and this layer will in due course flow into the Catholic Bible app beside the seed, so a reader of Tobias or Judith or 2 Machabees finds these threads at the foot of the page. Every added reference will be checked against both passages it joins, and labeled by kind — quotation, allusion, parallel account, or shared theme — and the dataset and the app will name their sources honestly as the work grows. Sources are public domain only: the biblical texts themselves (Sirach 44–50 and Wisdom 10 are built-in spines of parallels), public-domain commentaries and Fathers, and the seed above. No modern copyrighted apparatus is copied.
